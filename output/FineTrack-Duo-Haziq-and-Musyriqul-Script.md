# FineTrack Duo presentation script

For Haziq Farhan and Musyriqul Amin. Matched to the 25 slides currently defined in the FineTrack presentation. Suggested rehearsal target: 9 minutes 30 seconds, including pauses, transitions and demonstration. Times are targets, not measured delivery times.

Speak only the quoted paragraphs. Actions, purpose labels and timing notes are rehearsal guidance.

## How this meets the course requirements

| Requirement | Where it appears | How to deliver it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | Slides 1–2 | Ask a relatable question, identify the problem and introduce the proposed solution. |
| Technical definition | Slide 3 | State the term, class and distinguishing features. Then explain Bluetooth in everyday language. |
| Product description | Slides 4–12 and 14 | Describe appearance, shape, material, parts and their functions. Use the model and real device as evidence. |
| Process description | Slides 13 and 15–22 | Explain charging, attachment, pairing and finding in a clear sequence. Explain both user actions and what the system does. |
| Advantages and persuasion | Throughout, especially slides 14–17 and 23 | Connect each feature to a benefit for students or everyday users. |
| Objections and limitations | Slides 20–23 | Address platform compatibility, delayed updates and the limits of recovery honestly. |
| Conclusion and invitation | Slides 24–25 | Return to the opening problem, recommend the product to suitable users and invite questions. |

The Week 2 presentation assignment specifies pair work, an introduction, technical definition, technical description, advantages and conclusion, delivered within 8–10 minutes. It also asks students to obtain lecturer approval and bring the item. The revision PDF's four-paragraph, 300–350-word requirement belongs to its separate persuasive essay task. Its persuasive structure is useful here, but that word limit does not apply to this presentation.

Use the simple pattern **feature → function → benefit** when rehearsing. For example: “The corner opening holds a keyring, so you can keep the tracker attached to the keys you use every day.” Use present tense for product facts and sequence markers such as “first”, “next” and “finally” for processes. Say “can help” rather than promising guaranteed recovery.

## Slide-by-slide speaking script

### Slide 1 — Meet FineTrack

**Haziq · 35 seconds · Persuasive introduction**

Action: Face the audience. Hold up the real tracker and pause briefly after the question.

> “Good morning, Cik Wardah and everyone. Have you ever been ready to leave for class, but suddenly could not find your keys? That small problem can make your whole morning stressful. I’m Haziq Farhan, and my partner is Musyriqul Amin. Today, we’re introducing the UGREEN FineTrack Duo. We’ll explain its design, show how it works, and demonstrate why it could be useful in your daily routine.”

### Slide 2 — Join the presentation

**Haziq · 10 seconds · Audience engagement**

Action: Point to the QR code. Keep this short. Check that the destination opens before presenting.

> “You can scan this QR code to explore our presentation on your phone. For now, follow the main screen as we introduce the product.”

### Slide 3 — What it is

**Haziq · 40 seconds · Technical definition and explanation**

> “The UGREEN FineTrack Duo is a rechargeable Bluetooth tracking tag that helps users locate personal belongings through a compatible smartphone and finding network. Here, ‘tracking tag’ is its class, while its rechargeable power and Bluetooth connection describe its distinguishing features. Bluetooth is a short-range wireless connection. Nearby, you can use sound to help locate the tag. Farther away, a finding network can provide a reported location. It is not a continuous GPS tracker, so updates may not be immediate.”

### Slide 4 — All colours

**Haziq · 10 seconds · Product appearance**

> “Our model shows four looks. Graphite represents our reference finish. Pearl, Blue and Green are presentation concepts, rather than confirmed colours available to buy.”

### Slide 5 — Graphite

**Haziq · 10 seconds · Product shape**

Action: Point to the corners and attachment opening.

> “Notice the compact, roughly square body, rounded corners and opening near one corner. That opening provides a convenient attachment point.”

### Slide 6 — Pearl

**Haziq · 5 seconds · Appearance concept**

> “This Pearl concept shows the same design in a lighter finish.”

### Slide 7 — Blue

**Haziq · 5 seconds · Appearance concept**

> “The Blue concept changes the appearance while keeping the same shape.”

### Slide 8 — Green

**Haziq · 5 seconds · Transition to function**

> “Finally, the Green concept completes the preview. Let’s examine the useful parts.”

### Slide 9 — Explore the parts

**Haziq · 35 seconds · Product parts and functions**

Action: Select the casing, attachment opening, indicator and charging port in that order. Avoid spending time repeatedly rotating the model.

> “The casing houses and protects the electronics. The corner opening connects to a ring or strap. The front indicator provides a visible status signal, although the blinking shown here is only an illustration. At the bottom, the USB-C port supplies charging power. There is also a sounder inside the tracker. Each part has a purpose: carrying the device, powering it, or helping you locate your belongings.”

### Slide 10 — Casing material

**Haziq · 25 seconds · Material and benefit**

> “The enclosure is plastic. Plastic can provide a lightweight, electrically insulating housing, and it can be moulded into this rounded shape. For everyday use, that means a compact enclosure that holds the electronics together and is convenient to carry. These are general material benefits. We are not claiming a particular drop-resistance rating.”

### Slide 11 — Inside the layers

**Haziq · 35 seconds · Internal component functions**

Action: Show the separated assembly. Make the version distinction before describing the battery.

> “This illustration explains the parts inside a related tracker. It shows a coin-cell version, so it is not the exact internal design of the rechargeable Duo. The battery supplies electrical energy. The circuit board connects the electronic components, and the antenna supports Bluetooth communication. The sounder produces the locating alert. Together, these parts show how power, communication and sound support the finding process.”

### Slide 12 — Port covered

**Haziq · 15 seconds · Product detail and handover**

> “Returning to the Duo, this small plug covers the charging port and helps keep dirt out. It sits close to the casing. Musyriqul will now explain charging and everyday use.”

### Slide 13 — Open to charge

**Musyriqul · 20 seconds · Charging process**

Action: Show the real port if available. Do not invent a charging duration or indicator pattern.

> “Thank you, Haziq. First, remove the dust plug. Next, connect a suitable USB-C charging cable. Follow the supplied manual to check when charging is complete. Finally, disconnect the cable and press the plug back into place. The tracker is then ready for continued use.”

### Slide 14 — Battery & charging

**Musyriqul · 25 seconds · Specification and persuasive benefit**

> “UGREEN states that one full charge can provide up to one year of use. Actual battery life varies with usage. For someone who already has several devices to charge, less frequent charging is a useful advantage. USB-C recharging also means you can keep using the built-in battery without routinely buying replacement coin cells.”

### Slide 15 — On your keys

**Musyriqul · 15 seconds · Attachment process and use case**

Action: Hold up the tracker attached to keys, if available.

> “To use it with keys, pass a keyring through the corner opening. The tracker then stays with your keys. If you misplace them nearby, the sound feature can help guide your search.”

### Slide 16 — On your bag

**Musyriqul · 15 seconds · Attachment and user benefit**

> “For a bag, use a suitable ring or strap to attach it securely. Think about leaving your study bag at a café. A reported location can give you somewhere to begin looking.”

### Slide 17 — On your luggage

**Musyriqul · 15 seconds · Travel use case**

> “You can also attach it to luggage. Checking its reported location can provide a useful clue when travelling. However, it cannot guarantee recovery or provide continuous updates about a moving suitcase.”

### Slide 18 — Find your things

**Musyriqul · 35 seconds · Operating process**

> “The finding process has two situations. First, when the tracker is connected nearby, select your item in the finding app and request a sound. The tag produces an alert, and you follow it to your belongings. Second, when the item is farther away, participating devices in the selected finding network may detect the tag and help report its location. You check that location in the app, then use sound once you reconnect nearby.”

### Slide 19 — Connect to iPhone

**Musyriqul · 25 seconds · Setup overview**

Action: Select “Connect tracker”. Explain that this animation illustrates pairing; it does not pair a real tracker.

> “For a compatible iPhone, use Apple Find My. First, prepare the tracker for pairing according to its manual. Next, follow the app’s prompts to add the accessory and give it a useful name, such as ‘My Keys’. After setup, select that item when you need to find it. Our on-screen connection is a demonstration.”

### Slide 20 — Connect to Android

**Musyriqul · 25 seconds · Alternative setup and objection**

> “For a compatible Android phone, use Google Find Hub and follow its setup prompts. You might ask, ‘Can we track the same tag through both systems together?’ The answer is no. The Duo uses one finding platform at a time. The benefit is having a choice of platform, so check compatibility with your phone before buying.”

### Slide 21 — The tracking map

**Musyriqul · 25 seconds · Interpreting the result**

Action: Switch between Keys and Bag. Identify the map as a sample.

> “Here, selecting an item displays a sample reported location. In actual use, check when that location was updated. An older report is a clue, rather than proof that the item is still there. If no recent update appears, check your connection and app permissions. Further reports also depend on participating devices detecting the tracker.”

### Slide 23 — Why choose it

**Haziq · 30 seconds · Benefits and balanced recommendation**

> “So, why consider FineTrack Duo? Recharging supports repeated use, platform choice lets you match it to your compatible phone, and UGREEN lists no subscription fee for the finding service. The practical value is having another way to search when an important item goes missing. It cannot promise recovery every time, but for someone who regularly misplaces belongings, that extra help can make it worth considering.”

### Slide 24 — The takeaway

**Musyriqul · 25 seconds · Persuasive conclusion and call to action**

> “Remember the missing keys at the start? FineTrack Duo gives you a way to respond: check the app, locate a report, and listen nearby. If misplaced belongings regularly interrupt your day, consider choosing one for the item you lose most often. Check phone compatibility and the current seller’s price, then decide whether that convenience is worth it for you.”

### Slide 25 — Thank you

**Both · 10 seconds · Audience invitation**

> **Haziq:** “Thank you for listening. What would you attach yours to?”
>
> **Musyriqul:** “Your keys, your bag, or your luggage? We’re happy to take your questions.”

## Rehearsal and slide alignment notes

- Aim for approximately 4 minutes 45 seconds each, including your portions of the shared demonstration. Time a full rehearsal rather than judging balance by slide count.
- Keep slides 4–8 to about 35 seconds altogether. They show appearance, but the parts and operating process carry more explanatory value.
- Slide 11 shows a different battery configuration. Always say this before explaining the components. Do not describe its CR2032 coin cell as the Duo's rechargeable battery.
- The on-screen title “One design. Four looks.” refers to the presentation's concepts. Do not offer all four as retail choices.
- Slides 19 and 20 show alternative platforms. They are not consecutive steps that connect one tag to both systems.
- Slide 22 is an illustration of possible use, not a recorded product test or evidence of guaranteed recovery. The map positions are fictional.
- Do not add dimensions, weight, charging duration, battery capacity, waterproof claims or a Malaysian selling price unless verified for the actual unit you bring.
- Use the script to rehearse, then reduce each slide to a few cue words. The lecturer's presentation guidance emphasises audience contact rather than constantly reading notes.
- Bring the lecturer-approved device and practise pointing to its real parts. Keep the QR introduction brief even if the audience takes longer to scan.

## Short answers for likely questions

**Does it work with both iPhone and Android simultaneously?**

“No. It supports a compatible iPhone or Android platform, one at a time.”

**Is it a live GPS tracker?**

“No. It uses Bluetooth and a finding network. Distant reports depend on participating devices and may be delayed.”

**Is recovery guaranteed?**

“No. It provides tools that can help you search, but coverage, battery power and other conditions affect the result.”

**Why does your inside view show a coin cell?**

“It is an educational illustration based on a different tracker configuration. The FineTrack Duo we are discussing recharges through USB-C.”

**How much does it cost?**

“We have not verified a current Malaysian selling price. Please check the exact Duo model with the seller before purchasing.”
